3 Prototypes exploring Movement

Assignment

Prototyping, sept-nov 2026

Create 3 materialised prototypes that explore movement, without using glue. Document each prototype and the process of making

Tags

3D physical material study sketch Prototyping

Excited about the assignment, my first step was to just sit for a bit and write anything that came to me and could fit for the assingment. This took only a couple minutes, and I was satisfied with my list.

So, I Have the list of brainstorm ideas, and a few weeks to mull them over. In this period I instinctively wandered over to a select few of them, and thought them over, thinking about materials and implementations I'd use to actualize them. With that, once I sat down to start working them out in more detail, I had converged on the following: !!! images here of sketchbook !!!

My main inspiration for the bottle and chandelier were windchimes, the bottle focussing on up-close movement, and the chandelier focussing on a rooms passive airflow. The chandelier is actually directly based on a decoration I have had in my own room for many years. The balloon is inspired by the human body, and specifically the heart. While brainstorming I was attentive for the movement that was casually around me, and noticed the distinct inflation and deflation of our bodies that we push to the back of our senses. As a bonus, I can not say that the poster directly in view of my standard seat in class didn't influence me either. !!! inspiration pictures !!!! A likeness between them all is that they all center air movement. I came to this unintentionally, but some subconcious part of me must have honed in on the air that is of course always moving around us.

With the sketches, and after gathering some balloons, threads, leaves, twigs, bottles, and some miscalanious hardware, I put my first prototypes together. I made two versions of my bottle, to see and test some variables I wanted to eventually combine. I made the baloon prototype using a bottle as "lung" and some wool thread. I made a chandelier mockup in shapr3D. !!! images here of thingas !!!

With these prototypes I headed to a quick review section in class. I showed my ideas, and automatically while talking through my reasoning, I voices points of improvement I hadn't realized I felt. The following state my first instinctive feelings, and some expanded reasoning on why.

  • The inflation of the balloon wasn't significant enough. The model I had in mind to achieve, inflated/deflated to be the size of a head and the size of a fist, which with the "lung capacity" of only a bottle, is hard to achieve on the first try.
  • I was bothered by the lack of rigidity on the balloon structure. The thread I had quickly put together for this first prototype didn't hold up well on its own, and was too easily displaced by the movement of the balloon.
  • The bottle that had leaves and light material had its content too easily displaced my blowing into them. The bottle that had beads and metal had its content barely displaced by the air. Neither displayed the intermediate movement I intended.
