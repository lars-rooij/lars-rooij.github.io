3 Prototypes exploring Movement

Assignment

Prototyping, sept-nov 2026

Create 3 materialised prototypes that explore movement, without using glue. Document each prototype and the process of making

Tags

3D physical material study sketch Prototyping

Excited about the assignment, my first step was to just sit for a bit and write anything that came to me and could fit for the assingment. This took only a couple minutes, and I was satisfied with my list.

So, I Have the list of brainstorm ideas, and a few weeks to mull them over. In this period I instinctively wandered over to a select few of them, and thought them over, thinking about materials and implementations I'd use to actualize them. With that, once I sat down to start working them out in more detail, I had converged on the following:

My main inspiration for the bottle and chandelier were windchimes, the bottle focussing on up-close movement, and the chandelier focussing on a rooms passive airflow. The chandelier is actually directly based on a decoration I have had in my own room for many years. The balloon is inspired by the human body, and specifically the heart. While brainstorming I was attentive for the movement that was casually around me, and noticed the distinct inflation and deflation of our bodies that we push to the back of our senses. As a bonus, I can not say that the poster directly in view of my standard seat in class didn't influence me either.


A likeness between them all is that they all center air movement. I came to this unintentionally, but some subconcious part of me must have honed in on the air that is of course always moving around us.

With the sketches, and after gathering some balloons, threads, leaves, twigs, bottles, and some miscalanious hardware, I put my first prototypes together. I made two versions of my bottle, to see and test some variables I wanted to eventually combine. I made the baloon prototype using a bottle as "lung" and some wool thread. I made a chandelier mockup in shapr3D.

With these prototypes I headed to a quick review section in class. I showed my ideas, and automatically while talking through my reasoning, I voices points of improvement I hadn't realized I felt. The following state my first instinctive feelings, and some expanded reasoning on why.

  • The inflation of the balloon wasn't significant enough. The model I had in mind to achieve, inflated/deflated to be the size of a head and the size of a fist, which with the "lung capacity" of only a bottle, is hard to achieve on the first try.
  • I was bothered by the lack of rigidity on the balloon structure. The thread I had quickly put together for this first prototype didn't hold up well on its own, and was too easily displaced by the movement of the balloon.
  • The bottle that had leaves and light material had its content too easily displaced my blowing into them. The bottle that had beads and metal had its content barely displaced by the air. Neither displayed the intermediate movement I intended.

Chandelier

There were few things I wanted to itterate upon with the chandelier, the main next step in the process included making the 3D model physical. So, I saw down, and cut rough circles out of my colourfull paper. I attatched each to a black string, and threaded that through a piece of cardboard, its places spaced out beforehand. Lastly, I used sate-prikkers to suspend it.

Finishing this itteration, I found that the next should include several points of improvemnt:

  • The individual papers were too close together in my opinion. I want it to feel open and airy, and these hide behind eachother.
  • Adding upon the last point, I think the layers are too close together, and in further work want to create more depth. This would also go better if I used less busy papers. This choice was because I had these on hand and I usually like a bussier feel of things, but I think simpler is the way to go, to make the movement the central point of attention.
  • The black string distracts, and it would make watching the main papers easier if the string was clear or white.
  • For now I used tape for efficiency, but I think that goes against the 'no glue' rules, so the end goal is to tie it together instead of stick it with tape.

Human Heart

The last itteration showed me that the string I used didn't hold its shape well throughout the process, so I opted for a sturdier material with this iteration, some metal deco wire. This material was still bendable enough for me to shape it according to my vision, yet it holds its shape throughtout. I started with a base encapsulating the balloon at 4 points, and then strung another piece through those. Below are images comparing the balloon in the state of its two extremes.

Upon next itterations, I want to include other aspects that excentruate the movement, like strings getting put under stress by the expansion, or some bells that sound with the movement.

Contained Wind

With the last itteration I found that both concepts being combined would better fit my goal, as they either had too much influence from the wind or too little. So, in this itteration I strung my leaves and other bits from the 'top' of the bottle, and the movement fitted much better to my goal!

This itteration was a step to see if the combination would actually work like I expected, and I am pleased to see that it did. Next itterations, I want to find a larger bottle so that I can comfortably put more elements in, while keeping them all free to move. Additionally, this itteration also has beads strung from the 'ceiling', and I want to add more variation like that. I find that while the natural leaves and such are cute, perhaps immitation of it is better suited given the preservation aspects of them.

Peer feedback

With the above, I went to class and quickly introduced them to my peers. My main question was wether or not the movements I'm trying to explore are clear, and wether they would know how to interact with the human heart and contained wind. The response to these, and general comments on them, were the following:

  • Movements are clear!
  • I could see that the bottle was squeezed, and thus expect to squeeze the bottle!
  • Is there anything else that can blow for contained wind, to change up the interactivy?
  • Can you go bigger?
  • Why are you using paper for the chandelier?

I'm fairly content with these comments. I am glad my movements are clear enough, and that the methods of interaction are given through the affordances of the bottle. Regarding the questions, answering them gave me a clearer idea of what my own mind wanted.

  • I am choosing not to experiment with the creation of the movement for contained wind. I like the manual blowing, and how close a person has to be to the subject to experience it. A large part on why I chose to implement the blowing via straw is because it gives the person a lot of controle over positioning. Blowing with the straw tilted up versus down can change how things move. With the next itteration, I want to convey this purpose more clearly.
  • I can go bigger, and I will! I did not mention this before during the class, but as stated above, with the next itteration I want the chandelier and the contained wind to be larger. I am content with the size of the human heart, as I feel it matches the real life size.
  • Ideally, I would indeed have tried many different materials to base the chandelier around. I think paper is a really good fit, as it moves elegantly in free air, but without too much freedom. Nonetheless, it is a fair point, and something I should have explored more. Mentally, because of my inspiration for it, I was already set on paper throughout the process. I wish to challenge this more whenever I next need to prototype.
  • Not a question, but something I observed while a peer was interacting with contained wind: They twisted the bottle so as to not have to constantly put pressure on it. I had not considered this, but realize this is something I should take into account. Being able to consistently have it expanded without a grip on the bottle is very usefull for testing and other interactions.
