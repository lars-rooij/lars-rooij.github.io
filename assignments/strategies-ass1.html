Regarding Structuralism

Len Lye, A Colour Box (1935)

László Moholy-Nagy, Photogram (1922)

I view both Len Lye's A Color Box (1935) and László Moholy-Nagy's Photogram (1922) as examples of structuralist art. While being both made on photosensitive material, one in the format of a film and the other in the format of a photograph, neither follow the traditional format of their respective arts. Neither of them involve a camera at all.

Photogram is one work in a series of László Moholy-Nagy, where photosensitive material was directly exposed to light, instead of through the lens of a camera. The result is an interaction of merely a direct light, and the material. The piece shown was one of the first of its kind, and Moholy-Nagy was trying to establish light as a medium just like 'colour in paintings' and 'tone in music'. Since then, a 'photogram' has indeed become an established term, usually done by placing items directly onto the light-sensitive surface before exposure.

Similarly, A Color Box eliminates the camera, and is dependent on the film's material structure. Coloured materials were directly applied to the film strip, utilizing the way its displayed to create smooth and fluid movements.

As a result, I define both as fitting under structuralism, although they do so in different ways. A Color Box exploits the affordance of film, by the fact that it is a long singular strip that gets run through a projector to achieve its motion. Photogram exploits the workings of photosensitive materials, and the direct projection that is possible on them. In both cases, the exploitation of these affordances, and the focus on them, are made to be central and the subject of the works.

Moreover, there is a difference in the application that defines their structuralist method. Both are derivative of photography/film and have omitted the camera, but they have done so in a different way. László Moholy-Nagy has founded his structuralism in the application of light, whereas Len Lye has founded his structuralism in what light is applied upon. Lye shows what film can be when the photographed world is removed, while Moholy-Nagy shows what a photograph can be when the camera is removed.

Additionally, their temporal difference are also distinct. Critically to its methods, A Color Box is unable to be experienced unless it is run through a projector. It necessitates technology, time, a projection light, and a proper location to be viewed (before digitization came into play of course). On the other hand, Photogram, in its nature, requires at most that you are in the room it is displayed in. This difference is a consequence of their chosen medium over anything, but also does highlight the different ways in which structuralist art can showcase its own production.

Ultimately, both works demonstrate how structuralist art can emerge by stripping away the conventions of a medium and exposing its fundamental properties. Len Lye expands film into the movement of its material strip, while László Moholy-Nagy reduces photography to exposure. In doing so, both works shift attention strictly and straightforwardly towards the medium itself.
